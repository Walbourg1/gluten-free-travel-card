Gluten-free translation cards compared

Four options, four different answers — depending on what matters to you

Disclosure: I built one of the four options compared here (Gluten Free Travel Card). I have tried to be straight about where it loses, because a comparison you can't trust is worth nothing. Every claim below comes from the providers' own pages, checked in August 2026.

If you have celiac disease and travel, you will eventually look for a translation card. There are four established options, and the honest answer is that none of them is best at everything. They solve slightly different problems, and the right pick depends on which trade-off you care about.

How I compared them

Rather than declare an overall winner, here are the criteria and their honest trade-offs. Pick the row that matters most to you and the answer follows:

  • Translation verification — has a human who understands celiac disease checked it?
  • Number of languages — matters if you travel off the beaten track
  • Price
  • Format — printed card, plastic card, or app
  • Works offline — restaurant wifi is unreliable everywhere
  • Reads aloud — useful when staff would rather listen than read

Which one should you pick?

If you want the highest confidence in the translation

Legal Nomads. Each card is translated twice by native speakers who understand celiac disease and the local cross-contact risks. It covers the fewest languages and costs the most per language, and it is still the right choice for a trip where getting it wrong is not an option.

If you want a durable card that survives a backpack

Equal Eats. A credit-card-sized plastic card in 58 languages, with a documented three-tier translation process. Nothing to charge, nothing to unlock.

If you want free and the widest language coverage

CeliacTravel.com. 63 languages, free to download and print, funded by donations. It has been doing this for years. The translation process is not publicly documented, and you have to remember to print before you leave.

If you want it on your phone, offline, and spoken aloud

Gluten Free Travel Card — my own app. It is the only one of the four that is a real offline app rather than a document, and the only one that reads the card aloud in the local language. It is free with no ads, no account and no data collection.

The honest caveat: the translations are AI-generated and have not yet been reviewed by native speakers. National celiac associations have been asked to check their language, and this page will be updated as that happens. If verified translation quality is your priority, one of the other three is the better choice today.

What none of them replace

A card gets the message across; it does not guarantee a safe kitchen. Ask whether the pan and board are clean, expect flour in sauces, and be wary of shared fryers — the three risks that show up in every cuisine regardless of which card you hand over.
